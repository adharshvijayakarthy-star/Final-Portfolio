Follow the lanterns

01

The “Adharsh mentality”

Among people who know me, there is a small running phrase. When someone needs confidence, they sometimes call it an “Adharsh mentality”.

It is a narrow reputation in a narrow circle, and I want to describe it at that size rather than inflate it into something about influence. But it does tell me which part of me other people find useful.

02

Stop pleasing everyone

A friend I trust told me to stop being so formal, stop people-pleasing, and be more open. It was not gentle and it was not wrong.

I already knew. I just hadn't accepted it.

03

The coder I am, and the coder I want to be

I like calling myself a coder.

I'm not yet the coder I want to be.

Both of those are true at once, so here is the honest version.

  • Real projects have been built, and they work.
  • My code comprehension is stronger than my independent technical depth.
  • I have used AI assistance extensively, including vibe coding.
  • My Python is not yet at an intermediate level.
  • My ambition toward AI and ML is much larger than my current depth in it.

That's not an apology.

It's a gap.

I intend to close it.

04

Leading without a title

The MUN club ran every week because I kept building the thing that made it run. The house activity happened because a co-leader was absent and I stepped in to help organise it and manage the crowd.

Neither came with a position attached. What I take from both is smaller and more useful than a leadership claim: when something needs doing and I am in the room, I would rather be the one who starts. The courtyard has the fuller account.
