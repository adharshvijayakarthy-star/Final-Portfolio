The Leader

I don't think leadership starts with a title.

Primary evidence · MUN Club

I built a weekly MUN club for younger students, and then I ran it.

I designed the lessons. I wrote the debates and the simulations. I taught the sessions, and I built the structure the club needed in order to happen every week rather than once.

Nobody handed me a programme to deliver. The programme was the work.

Secondary evidence · school house activity

A co-leader was absent, so the job was open.

I stepped into the responsibility: helping organise the activity and managing the crowd while it ran. It was not a title and it was not planned. It was simply the thing that needed doing, and I was there.

Communication is how I turn what I know into something other people can use.

The shadow side

When something isn't getting done, my instinct is to do it myself.

That instinct gets things finished. It also quietly removes the chance for someone else to grow into the job. I am still learning when ownership means stepping up, and when it means stepping back and letting someone else step up instead.
